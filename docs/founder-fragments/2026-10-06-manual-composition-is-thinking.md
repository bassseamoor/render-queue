## Derived principle

Manual composition is not merely a slower transport for a finished thought. The friction of choosing letters, words, sentence order, omissions, corrections, and emphasis is itself part of cognition.

Fast capture and AI expansion are valuable when the objective is throughput. They should not automatically replace deliberate composition when the objective is forming, testing, or deepening the idea.

A useful MOOR loop is:

1. The human manually constructs the thought.
2. The system preserves that raw version exactly.
3. AI reflects, expands, challenges, or structures it.
4. The human notices gaps or misprojection.
5. The next manual pass incorporates what was learned.
6. Both the raw lineage and later interpretations remain available.

## Product implication

MOOR should optimize separately for **capture speed** and **thinking quality**.

Do not assume the fastest input method is the best input method.

Where useful, preserve:
- original manually typed text;
- model-expanded interpretation;
- later corrections;
- which ideas changed after reflection;
- the resulting principle or decision.

The goal is not to remove productive friction. It is to remove useless friction while preserving the friction that makes the user think.

## Why the GPT chat pattern matters

The current chat interaction is useful because it provides an immediate destination for a manually-built thought. The user can construct an idea under friction, receive a reflection, see what was misunderstood or missing, and continue building without losing the original sequence.

That makes the conversation less like prompt automation and more like a cognitive workbench.
