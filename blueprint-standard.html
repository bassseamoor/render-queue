MOOR doctrine v1

The Blueprint
Standard

Every blueprint sells the idea, specs the build, and shows the craft. Miss one and it's not a blueprint — it's a memo, a poster, or a daydream.
Pillar 01

SELL

Make the reader want the thing built. The promise, the stakes, the click. A blueprint nobody wants is a failed blueprint.

Pillar 02

SPEC

Detail it so a builder executes to desired spec from the blueprint alone. Parts, steps, interfaces, acceptance. LEGO-manual clarity.

Pillar 03

SHOW

Show the experience. A blueprint must SHOW everything building it will do — visual reference of the experience itself, not just numbers. Numbers are complimentary; the seeing is the selling. Render it with visible skill and drama. Colorful, detailed, premium. The craft must show on the page.

01

Sell it

Blueprints are the funnel's flowers. A flower doesn't do the pollinating — its color and scent draw the pollinator who does the work. A blueprint is the attractive display that draws the build into existence.

The sell is not hype — it is the honest case. Every blueprint opens with three things:

1

The promise

What the thing is, in one breath. No preamble.

2

The stakes

What changes if it exists. What stays broken if it doesn't.

3

The click

The moment the reader sees it working in their head. No click — the blueprint isn't ready.

02

Spec it

A competent builder executes to desired spec from the blueprint alone. No side conversations, no guessing. Required in every blueprint:

1

Parts list

Every component, named, with its role.

2

Build steps

Ordered, numbered, each with a done-state.

3

Interfaces

How parts connect — inputs, outputs, contracts.

4

Acceptance

How the builder and the verifier know each step is done. Observable, checkable, no vibes.

5

Non-goals

What the thing explicitly does NOT do. The honest boundary is part of the spec.

03

Show it

Color with intent. Accent palette per section; color carries meaning, not decoration.

Drama in layout. Hero, beats, reveals. The page has rhythm — it never reads like a memo.

Detail density. Diagrams, callouts, annotated structure. A blueprint rewards zooming in.

Visible skill. Accumulated knowledge and tools on display. The reader feels the maker behind it.

The exemplar rule: the standard's own page embodies the standard. Any doctrine that demands craft must demonstrate craft. This page is the proof.
